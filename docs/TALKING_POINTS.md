# Talking points — CurbFusion, Team Trash Pandas

Quick-reference for the pitch and Q&A. Organized by judging category so you can grab the right
line under pressure. Full demo script/checklist is in [DEMO.md](DEMO.md); this is the soundbite layer.

## The 15-second hook

> "The city publishes garbage-day polygons, missed pickups, dead-animal calls, tree-trim requests,
> and traffic counts. None of them mention raccoons. Fuse them together and they answer the
> question anyway: Wednesday 10pm, Heights side streets — best night of the week to be a raccoon."

Then flip to Curb mode, same hour, same faces: pressure moves to Washington Ave's commercial
blocks instead. **Same 18 datasets, same ~3,000 block faces, we changed seven numbers.** That one
flip is the whole pitch in ten seconds — one engine, two completely different answers.

## Technical Execution & Completeness (30 pts)

- **18+ live public datasets**, one common unit: block face × hour of week. HCAD parcels, COH
  road centerlines, residential permit parking, Public Works ADT, METRO GTFS, 311 service
  requests, Solid Waste pickup/missed-collection/dead-animal layers, FEMA flood zones, bikeways,
  parks, traffic control points.
- **Nothing fabricated.** Every constant is in `data/core/assumptions.json` with an ID and a
  note; every source URL, pull date, and count is in `data/core/manifest.json`. Confidence is
  scored per block face, not asserted globally.
- **Performance, proven, not claimed:** the largest corridor (Fifth Ward, 3,634 block faces)
  loads in 160ms and renders every animation frame in ~9ms — smooth well past a single demo
  street, verified under load, not just at the zoom level of a screenshot.
- Three working, cross-linked apps, zero setup: **Map**, **Recipe Lab**, **Recipe Agent** — open
  by double-click, no server required.

## Track Fit — Houston Open Data (20 pts)

- This could not be entered in another track unchanged — it doesn't exist without Houston's own
  open data. Say that sentence out loud; it's the whole criterion.
- **Nine real corridors, not one demo street:** all six historic Houston wards (First through
  Sixth) plus three commercial corridors (Westheimer/Montrose, 19th St/Heights, Main
  St/Midtown) — built from the same generalized, one-command pipeline against live
  Houston/ArcGIS endpoints, no auth, no synthetic data.
- If asked "does this scale beyond your demo block": yes, and here's the command that proves it
  (`build_new_corridor.py <slug> "<Label>" <bbox>`) — adding Houston's next corridor is a single
  script call, not a rewrite.

## Value & Impact (25 pts)

- **Insight Quality:** the same 18 datasets that describe curb-parking conflict also describe
  where a raccoon forages best — nobody looks at those two questions together, and they turn out
  to be the same math with different weights.
- **Usability — built to be picked up, not decoded:** search any Houston address, drag a slider
  and watch the week go by, get a plain-English sentence instead of a raw score ("High curb
  pressure — permit hours are in effect right now; the block is mostly commercial"), and click
  **"Copy briefing"** to get a pasteable, plain-text block report — score, reasoning, land use,
  pickup schedule, 311 activity, a live link back — ready for an email or a 311 follow-up today,
  not a dashboard you have to interpret for someone else.
- **Real, named customers, not "everyone":** ParkHouston and district managers (curb pressure),
  Solid Waste ops (complaint stacking), food trucks and delivery logistics (where to stop),
  homebuyers (quiet + dry). The raccoon is the proof the framework is need-agnostic — if it can
  answer for a raccoon, it can answer for anyone.

## Frontier Factor (25 pts)

- **Recipe system:** a recipe is just signed weights over a shared component catalog plus a time
  window. Curb pressure and Raccoon forage are two of *seven* recipes shipping today (delivery
  driver, food truck Friday, homebuyer quiet-and-dry, city ops...).
- **Recipe Lab:** drag sliders over the same catalog, watch the map recolor live — anyone can
  compose their own lens without touching code.
- **Recipe Agent — the frontier line judges will remember:** describe a need in one sentence
  ("delivery driver with a box truck, mornings, hates tickets") and an LLM proposes the weights,
  *with a one-line reason per dataset it picked*, the time window it inferred, and an honest
  wishlist of data it wanted but doesn't have (ParkHouston meter transactions, TABC licenses).
  That wishlist is the ask, generated by the system itself, not written after the fact.
- **What-if scenarios:** toggle "permit hours applied every night" and pressure recomputes
  client-side, instantly, off the same formula — this isn't a static map, it's a light-weight
  policy simulator.

## If asked (from DEMO.md, still true)

- **"Is this real data?"** Every layer is a public city/county/federal endpoint, listed in
  `data/core/manifest.json`. Nothing synthetic.
- **"How accurate?"** Uncalibrated by design — every score says so. `confidence` is per face.
  Meter transactions (not public) would turn this from a model into a measurement; that's the ask.
- **"Surveillance?"** No plate readers, by choice — on the catalog page as explicitly excluded.
- **"Business model?"** Catalog and recipes are free; a licensed-data tier (meter transactions,
  foot traffic, satellite imagery) brokers through the same picker judges just saw.
- **"Does it only work for Washington Ave?"** No — curb pressure runs on all nine corridors.
  Raccoon mode and the recipe system currently have deep data for Washington Ave only; the UI
  says so explicitly rather than faking numbers elsewhere, and extending it is the team's
  next PR, not a rewrite.

## Closing line

> "Every constant we used is logged. Every source is public. The one thing standing between this
> being a model and being a measurement is data the city already has. That's the ask."
